# Compliance and safety requirements (data model and flows)

Retrieved: 2026-10-29

**TL;DR.** We have to design as if every piece of user nutrition data is regulated health data. Washington's My Health My Data Act (MHMDA, RCW 19.373) has no size threshold; small businesses only got a later start date. It defines "consumer health data" broadly enough to cover meal logs, weight, supplements, mentions of medications, pregnancy or conditions, and anything we infer from them. It requires consent that is opt-in, specific and separate from general terms, and the consent request has to name four things. It also requires a separate Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy linked from the homepage, and gives users access, withdrawal and deletion rights: 45 days to respond (one 45-day extension), deletion reaching processors and backups (backups within 6 months), and an appeal process. Violations are per se Consumer Protection Act violations, so private plaintiffs can sue. Nevada SB 370 (NRS 603A.400–.550) is similar but narrower, and only the Attorney General enforces it. The FTC's amended Health Breach Notification Rule (HBNR) very likely applies to us. FTC guidance uses a diet app that pulls calorie data from an API as its example of a covered "personal health record", and "diet" is listed by name in the rule. Under the HBNR, sharing data without authorization counts as a breach. HIPAA does not apply to a direct-to-consumer wellness service. On messaging, the TCPA, Washington's commercial-text statute (RCW 19.190) and CTIA norms are the conservative baseline. Users text first, so replies are conversational. Proactive messages need opt-in per category, and promotional texts (such as Stripe upsells) need express advance consent. STOP-family keywords must be honored, with at most one confirmation, sent within 5 minutes. No court decision on iMessage specifically was found. The Eating-disorder (ED) helpline we should share is the National Alliance for Eating Disorders: 1-866-662-1235, Mon–Fri 9am–7pm ET. It is not a crisis line, so we also give 988 and text "ALLIANCE" to 741741. Default calorie floors are 1,200 kcal (women) and 1,500 kcal (men). The default loss cap is 1–2 lb (0.5–1 kg) per week. Both need owner review. Stay inside FDA "general wellness" wording: weight management is fine; "treating obesity" or "treating an eating disorder" is not.

## 1. Washington My Health My Data Act (RCW 19.373; HB 1155, ch. 191, Laws of 2023)

Source text: the official session law (`1155-S.SL.pdf`), read verbatim from the wa-law.org GitHub mirror (tannewt/wa-law.org). Section numbers below are session-law sections. The RCW numbering is my mapping and may be off by one: Sec. 3 → 19.373.010, Sec. 4 → .020, Sec. 5 → .030, Sec. 6 → .040, Sec. 7 → .050, Sec. 8 → .060, Sec. 9 → .070, Sec. 10 → .080, Sec. 11 → .090, Sec. 12 → .100. **Confirm the current RCW text and section numbers on app.leg.wa.gov before citing them in the policy.**

### 1.1 Does diet, nutrition or weight data count as "consumer health data" (CHD)?
- **Definition, Sec. 3(8)(a):** "personal information that is linked or reasonably linkable to a consumer and that identifies the consumer's past, present, or future physical or mental health status." [primary]
- **Non-exhaustive list, Sec. 3(8)(b), with the items that matter to us:** (i) individual health conditions, treatment, diseases, or diagnosis; (ii) "social, psychological, behavioral, and medical interventions"; (iv) use or purchase of prescribed medication; (v) "bodily functions, vital signs, symptoms, or measurements of the information described in this subsection"; (viii) reproductive or sexual health information (for example, pregnancy); (ix) biometric data; (xii) "data that identifies a consumer seeking health care services"; (xiii) information derived or extrapolated from nonhealth information "by any means, including algorithms or machine learning." [primary]
- The statute does not name diet, nutrition or weight. [primary] The AG FAQ's example is that "an app that tracks someone's digestion or perspiration is collecting consumer health data," while buying toilet paper or deodorant is not. [search: atg.wa.gov FAQ snippet] Body weight is a bodily measurement. A coaching program is arguably a "behavioral intervention". Meal logs, calorie and protein targets and supplement stacks are the inputs we use to infer health status. Mentions of meds, pregnancy or conditions are squarely covered.
- **"Health care services," Sec. 3(15),** is "any service provided to a person to assess, measure, improve, or learn about a person's mental or physical health," including "bodily functions, vital signs, symptoms, or measurements." [primary] On that reading, even the fact that a number uses a nutrition coach could be argued to be CHD under (8)(b)(xii). This is my interpretation, not a holding.
- **Design conclusion:** treat all of the following as CHD: the profile (goal, height, weight, dietary restrictions), meal photos, food logs, macros and totals, supplement stack, check-in content, message bodies, model outputs about the user, and the phone-number-to-service relationship.
- **Biometric data, Sec. 3(4):** includes facial imagery "from which an identifier template can be extracted." [primary] Meal photos may contain faces. We must not run face recognition or extract templates.
- **Consumer, Sec. 3(7):** (a) a Washington resident, or (b) a natural person whose CHD is "collected in Washington"; individual or household context only. [primary] That second prong could reach a non-resident texting from inside Washington. **Design: apply WA rules to all users. Do not geo-segment.**
- **Personal information, Sec. 3(18):** includes data tied to a persistent unique identifier. It excludes deidentified data that meets the three-part test in Sec. 3(10): reasonable measures, a public commitment, and contractual obligations on recipients. [primary]

### 1.2 Who is covered, and are there thresholds?
- **Regulated entity, Sec. 3(23):** any legal entity that conducts business in WA *or* targets products or services to WA consumers, and that determines the purpose and means of processing CHD. [primary]
- **Small business, Sec. 3(28):** a regulated entity that collects, processes, sells or shares CHD of **fewer than 100,000 consumers** in a calendar year, *or* gets **less than 50% of gross revenue** from CHD and handles CHD of **fewer than 25,000 consumers**. [primary] Small businesses get the **same obligations**; only their start date differed (June 30, 2024). **There is no exemption threshold.** [primary]
- **Processor, Sec. 3(21) and Sec. 8:** may process CHD only under a **binding contract** that sets processing instructions and limits. A processor acting outside the contract becomes a regulated entity for that data. [primary] Anthropic, Sendblue (or Linq or Blooio), the Postgres host, Vercel and Blob storage, and Stripe all need contracts or DPAs whose terms match our CHD policy. Sec. 4(1)(e) also makes it a violation to contract with a processor in a way that is inconsistent with our CHD privacy policy. [primary]
- **Exemptions, Sec. 12:** HIPAA PHI, GLBA, FCRA, FERPA, IRB research, and others; none applies to us. Sec. 12(3) allows processing to prevent or respond to security incidents, fraud, harassment, malicious or illegal activity, but the entity carries the burden of showing the exemption applies (Sec. 12(4)). [primary]

### 1.3 Consent
- **Definition, Sec. 3(6)(a):** "a clear affirmative act that signifies a consumer's freely given, specific, informed, opt-in, voluntary, and unambiguous agreement, which may include written consent provided by electronic means." [primary]
- **Consent cannot be obtained by, Sec. 3(6)(b):** (i) acceptance of "a general or broad terms of use agreement or a similar document that contains descriptions of personal data processing along with other unrelated information"; (ii) hovering, muting, pausing or closing content; (iii) "deceptive designs." [primary] Deceptive design, Sec. 3(9), is a UI that subverts or impairs user autonomy. [primary]
- **Collection, Sec. 5(1)(a):** collection is allowed only (i) with consent "for such collection for a specified purpose," or (ii) "to the extent necessary to provide a product or service that the consumer ... has requested." [primary]
- **Sharing, Sec. 5(1)(b):** sharing is allowed only (i) with consent "that is **separate and distinct** from the consent obtained to collect," or (ii) to the extent necessary to provide the requested product or service. [primary]
- **What "share" excludes, Sec. 3(27)(b):** disclosure to a **processor** "to provide goods or services in a manner consistent with the purpose for which the consumer health data was collected and disclosed to the consumer"; certain direct-relationship third parties; and M&A transfers. [primary] If our only recipients are contracted processors acting within the disclosed purpose, we do not "share", and no separate sharing consent is needed. **Any future non-processor disclosure, such as a dietitian partner or a research partner, needs its own separate consent.**
- **Timing and contents of the consent request, Sec. 5(1)(c):** consent must be obtained **prior to** collection or sharing. The request must "clearly and conspicuously disclose": **(i)** the categories of CHD collected or shared; **(ii)** the purpose, "including the specific ways in which it will be used"; **(iii)** the categories of entities it is shared with; **(iv)** how to withdraw consent from future collection or sharing. [primary]
- **New categories or purposes, Sec. 4(1)(c)–(d):** collecting or using categories or purposes not in the CHD privacy policy requires disclosing them first and getting fresh affirmative consent. [primary] **Design: version the policy, and re-prompt for consent when categories or purposes expand.**
- **Non-discrimination, Sec. 5(1)(d):** we may not unlawfully discriminate against a consumer for exercising rights. [primary]
- **Withdrawal, Sec. 6(1)(b):** consumers have the right to withdraw consent to collection and sharing. [primary]

### 1.4 Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy
- **Required contents, Sec. 4(1)(a), disclosed "clearly and conspicuously":** (i) categories of CHD collected and the purpose, "including how the data will be used"; (ii) categories of **sources**; (iii) categories of CHD **shared**; (iv) "a list of the categories of third parties and **specific affiliates**" it is shared with; (v) how a consumer can exercise the Sec. 6 rights. [primary]
- **Homepage link, Sec. 4(1)(b):** "shall prominently publish a link to its consumer health data privacy policy on its homepage." [primary] The AG FAQ says it must be a **separate and distinct link**, and that the policy may not contain information the Act does not require. [search: AG FAQ summary]
- **"Homepage", Sec. 3(16):** "the introductory page of an internet website **and any internet webpage where personal information is collected**." For an online service, "the application's platform page or download page, and a link within the application, such as from the application configuration, 'about,' 'information,' or settings page." [primary] **Design:** put the link on the marketing site homepage and on any web page that collects data, including the Stripe Checkout return page and any web forms. In-thread, send it in the onboarding consent message and the HELP reply. I am extrapolating "a link within the application" to the iMessage thread.

### 1.5 Consumer rights (Sec. 6)
- **Access, (1)(a):** confirmation of whether we collect, share or sell, plus access to the data, "including a list of **all** third parties and affiliates with whom" we shared or sold it, with "an active email address or other online mechanism" for contacting them. [primary]
- **Deletion, (1)(c):** the consumer exercises it by "informing" us. We must **(A)** delete "from all parts of the ... network, including archived or backup systems," and **(B)** notify **all affiliates, processors, contractors, and other third parties** we shared CHD with. Those recipients must also delete, under (1)(c)(ii). **Backups, (1)(c)(iii):** deletion may be delayed to allow restoration of archived or backup systems, but "may not exceed **six months** from authenticating the deletion request." [primary]
- **Request channel, (1)(d):** a "secure and reliable means" that matches how consumers normally interact with us. We **may not require creating a new account**, but may require use of an existing one. [primary] The iMessage thread from the registered number is the natural channel.
- **Authentication, (1)(e):** if we cannot authenticate the request with commercially reasonable efforts, we need not comply and may ask for more information. [primary]
- **Fees, (1)(f):** free **up to twice annually per consumer**. We may charge a fee or decline for manifestly unfounded, excessive or repetitive requests, and we carry the burden of proof. [primary]
- **Deadline, (1)(g):** "without undue delay, but in all cases within **45 days of receipt**." It may be extended **once by 45 days** if we notify the consumer within the first 45 days with a reason. Authentication does not pause the clock. [primary]
- **Appeals, (1)(h):** must be conspicuously available. We respond in writing within **45 days** of the appeal, with reasons. If we deny it, we must give an online mechanism or other method for complaining to the **Attorney General**. [primary]
- **Data security, Sec. 7:** least-privilege access for employees, processors and contractors, and administrative, technical and physical safeguards meeting the industry's reasonable standard of care. [primary]

### 1.6 Sale, geofencing, enforcement, dates
- **Sale, Sec. 9:** selling, or offering to sell, CHD requires a **signed "valid authorization"**. It must be separate from collection and sharing consent and cover nine elements: the specific data; seller and purchaser names and contacts; purpose; a statement that service is not conditioned on signing; the right to revoke; a statement about redisclosure; an expiration **one year** from signing; and the signature and date. A copy goes to the consumer. Seller and purchaser keep it for **six years**. Sale is defined in Sec. 3(26) as an exchange "for monetary or other valuable consideration." [primary] **We never sell (spec), and must avoid ad pixels or SDKs that could be argued to be "valuable consideration."**
- **Geofencing, Sec. 10:** unlawful for "any person" to geofence (a virtual boundary of 2,000 ft or less, Sec. 3(14)) around an in-person health care provider to track consumers, collect CHD, or send notifications or ads. [primary] Effective **July 23, 2023**: it has no delayed date and took the 90-day default; one source says July 22. [search] We collect no location, so the risk is nil. We only need a policy statement.
- **Enforcement, Sec. 11:** a violation is an unfair or deceptive act under the **Consumer Protection Act (ch. 19.86 RCW)**, enforced by the AG and **private plaintiffs**. [primary] Private damages under RCW 19.86.090: actual damages, which the court may treble, with the trebled amount capped at **$25,000**. AG civil penalty under RCW 19.86.140: up to **$7,500 per violation**. [search; verify the current RCW text] The first class action was *Maxwell v. Amazon.com*, No. 2:25-cv-00261 (W.D. Wash., filed Feb. 10, 2025), over SDK location data. [search]
- **Effective dates:** Secs. 4–9 applied from **March 31, 2024** for regulated entities and **June 30, 2024** for small businesses. [primary] Both dates have passed, so everything applies to us at launch.
- **Amendments:** I found no enacted amendment to ch. 19.373. HB 1671 (2025–26), a comprehensive privacy bill that cross-references 19.373, exists only as introduced and substitute versions in the mirror, with no session law. [primary: mirror contents; search: no enactment found] **Re-check before launch.**

## 2. Nevada SB 370 (NRS 603A.400–603A.550)

I could not read the official text (leg.state.nv.us was blocked, and I found no verbatim mirror). Section titles come from the Justia NRS ch. 603A table of contents, mirrored in the CloudSecurityAlliance-DataSets repo. Each one is marked "[Effective March 31, 2024.]" [primary: TOC only] Everything else is [search].

- **Structure, from the TOC titles:** 603A.400–.485 definitions ("Consumer health data" is defined in .430); .490 applicability; **.495** privacy policy that must be "posted on Internet website"; **.500** collection and sharing prohibited without consent, plus "required disclosures for request for consent"; **.505/.510** consumer requests, response, authentication, fees; **.515** deletion, including deletion by third parties and the "delay of deletion of data on archived or backup system"; **.520** appeals; **.525** access limits and security; **.530** processors; **.535** sale requires authorization, which expires, can be revoked, and must be retained; **.540** geofence near in-person health care providers; **.545** non-discrimination; **.550** "Violation constitutes deceptive trade practice; **no private right of action**." [primary: TOC]
- **Effective date:** **March 31, 2024.** Signed June 15 or 16, 2023 (sources differ). [search]
- **CHD definition, narrower than WA:** personal information "linked or reasonably capable of being linked to a consumer" that a regulated entity **uses to identify** the consumer's past, present or future health status. It is a use-based test, but "bodily functions, vital signs or symptoms" and their monitoring or measurement are listed inclusions. [search] Our use, estimating intake against health-related goals, almost certainly meets the test.
- **Thresholds:** none. There is no revenue or consumer-count threshold and no small-business tier. [search]
- **Privacy policy (.495):** must clearly and conspicuously cover several items, among them: categories of CHD collected; the process for reviewing and requesting changes; how consumers are told of material changes; whether third parties may track users across sites; and the effective date. There must be a **hyperlink posted conspicuously on the main Internet website**. No new categories, third parties or purposes without disclosure and "affirmative, voluntary consent." The full enumerated list was **not retrieved**, and one draft also required the "categories of sources." [search] **Action: get the full .495 list before writing the CHD notice.**
- **Consent (.500):** must be **affirmative, voluntary** and obtained before collection or sharing. Collection and sharing consents are **separate and distinct**. The request must disclose categories, purpose and manner of use, and other items not fully retrieved. [search]
- **Requests (.510):** respond within **45 days after authentication**, extendable by **45 days** with notice and reasons before the original deadline. [search] This differs from WA, where the clock runs from **receipt**. **Design: use WA's stricter receipt-based clock for everyone.**
- **Deletion (.515):** third parties must delete within **30 days** of notice. **Backup delay conflict:** the original bill and first reprint said ≤6 months; the second and third reprints and the **enrolled** version reportedly say "not more than **2 years** after the request is authenticated." [search] WA's 6-month limit is stricter, so design to 6 months.
- **Sale (.535):** requires written authorization in plain language naming the seller and purchaser. The one-year expiry is not confirmed. [search]
- **Geofence (.540):** prohibited within **1,750 feet** of in-person health care facilities, for tracking, collecting CHD, or messaging. [search]
- **Enforcement (.550):** AG only, as a deceptive trade practice; **no private right of action**. [primary: TOC title] A civil penalty of up to **$5,000 per violation** comes from a single secondary source. [search; UNVERIFIED]
- **2025 changes:** SB 63 (2025, children under 13) cites the 603A.400–.550 range, but reportedly had "no further action" after June 3, 2025. I found no amendment to the CHD sections. [search; UNVERIFIED]

**WA vs NV, in short:** WA is broader in definition and scope (geofence by "any person"), lets private plaintiffs sue, runs the 45-day clock from receipt, caps backup delay at 6 months, and explicitly bans consent via general terms or deceptive design. NV is use-based, AG-only, runs the clock from authentication, and reportedly allows 2 years for backups. **A single WA-compliant implementation satisfies NV, provided the CHD notice also carries the NV-specific policy items from .495.**

## 3. FTC: Health Breach Notification Rule, Section 5, and ad-sharing

### 3.1 HBNR (16 CFR Part 318, as amended in 2024)
Text read from a verbatim eCFR snapshot (2025-02-06), mirrored at lucas-amberg/ecfr-analyzer. [primary] The final rule was published in the Federal Register on 2024-05-30 (doc 2024-10855) and took effect **2024-07-29**. [search]
- **Vendor of personal health records:** an entity, other than a HIPAA covered entity or business associate, "that offers or maintains a personal health record." (§318.2) [primary]
- **Personal health record:** "an electronic record of PHR identifiable health information on an individual that **has the technical capacity to draw information from multiple sources** and that is managed, shared, and controlled by or primarily for the individual." (§318.2) [primary]
- **PHR identifiable health information:** relates to physical or mental health and identifies the individual. It is "created or received by a ... **covered health care provider**," and "includes information that is provided by or on behalf of the individual." (§318.2) [primary]
- **Covered health care provider** includes "any other entity furnishing health care services or supplies". **Health care services or supplies** means "any online service such as a website, mobile application, or internet-connected device that provides mechanisms to track ... vital signs, symptoms, bodily functions, fitness, ... sleep, mental health, genetic information, **diet**, or that provides other health-related services or tools." (§318.2) [primary] The FTC explained that this covers wellness as well as medical services. [search]
- **Multiple-sources criterion:** coverage turns on technical capacity, not on what a given user actually connects. Only one source needs to supply health information; the other source can be non-health data. The FTC's examples are "Diet and Fitness App Y" pulling calendar data through an API, and a depression app that can sync a sleep monitor. A health-advice site that draws only from the consumer is not covered. [search: FR 2024-10855]
- **FTC compliance guidance** uses a **diet app that takes the user's weight entries and pulls restaurant-menu calorie counts through an API** as an example where "there's a good chance you have a PHR covered by the FTC's Rule." [search: ftc.gov "Complying with FTC's Health Breach Notification Rule"]
- **Application to us:** we take user inputs (text, photos, weight, goals) *and* draw data from the USDA FoodData Central API, which is our analog of the restaurant-calorie API. The record is managed primarily for the user. **Conclusion: assume we are a vendor of PHRs.** Our providers (Anthropic, Sendblue, the DB host) would be "third party service providers" under §318.3(b). [primary text + my analysis]
- **Breach of security:** "acquisition of such information without the authorization of the individual". It "includes an unauthorized acquisition ... that occurs as a result of a data breach **or an unauthorized disclosure**." Unauthorized access is presumed to be acquisition unless we have reliable contrary evidence. (§318.2) [primary] Sharing with an ad platform or analytics vendor beyond what the user authorized is a breach. GoodRx was the first case on this theory. [search]
- **Who and when (§318.3–318.4):** notify affected individuals, the **FTC**, and (if **≥500 residents of a state**) **prominent media** serving that state. Notice goes out "without unreasonable delay and in no case later than **60 calendar days**" after discovery. For ≥500 individuals, FTC notice is contemporaneous with individual notice. For <500, keep a log and send it to the FTC within 60 days after year-end. Discovery is the first day the breach is known, or reasonably should have been known, to any employee or agent. The burden of proof is on us (§318.4(c)). [primary]
- **Method (§318.5):** written notice to the last known address, or "electronic mail" **if the individual specified email as primary contact method**. **"Electronic mail" means "email in combination with one or more of: text message, within-application messaging, or electronic banner"** (§318.2). Otherwise, first-class mail. If contact information is insufficient for **10 or more** people: **substitute notice**, either a conspicuous homepage posting for **90 days** or major print or broadcast media, **plus a toll-free number active ≥90 days**. Urgent cases may add phone "or other means" (§318.5(a)(3)). [primary] **We hold only phone numbers, and text alone is not a permitted primary method, so substitute notice is our realistic fallback.**
- **Content (§318.6):** what happened, the date of the breach and of discovery, and the identity of third parties that acquired data; the types of data, including "the individual's use of a health-related mobile application"; steps to protect oneself; what we are doing; and **≥2 contact methods** (toll-free number, email, website, within-application, or postal address). [primary] Notices must be "clear and conspicuous" (§318.2 definition). [primary]
- **Penalties (§318.7):** treated as a violation of an FTC trade regulation rule, so civil penalties apply. FTC guidance cites up to **$53,088 per violation**. The 2026 inflation adjustment was **cancelled** (OMB had no Oct-2025 CPI-U), so the 2025 amounts still apply. [search] Past enforcement under the HBNR: GoodRx **$1.5M** (2023) and Easy Healthcare/Premom **$100,000** (2023). [search]
- The FTC **withdrew** its 2021 Policy Statement on health apps in **Sept 2026** as obsolete, because the amended Rule now covers health apps directly. [search]
- **Preemption, §318.1(b):** preempts state law as set out in ARRA §13421. [primary] This does not displace MHMDA's separate obligations. My reading; not verified.

### 3.2 FTC Act Section 5 health claims
- The FTC's **Health Products Compliance Guidance (Dec. 2022)** says claims about the health benefits or safety of foods, supplements and other health products need "**competent and reliable scientific evidence**" ("tests, analyses, research, or studies ... conducted and evaluated in an objective manner by experts ..."). RCTs are generally expected for health-benefit claims. [search: ftc.gov]
- **Implication:** marketing copy must not promise weight-loss results, calorie-estimate accuracy ("exact macros from a photo"), or disease outcomes unless we can substantiate them. In-product, present estimates as estimates with confidence levels; the spec already does this.

### 3.3 FTC stance on sharing health data with advertisers
- **GoodRx (2023):** a permanent ban on sharing health data with third parties for advertising, plus a $1.5M HBNR civil penalty, over sharing with Facebook, Google and Criteo. [search]
- **BetterHelp (final order July 14, 2023):** **$7.8M**; a ban on sharing health data for advertising; **affirmative consent** before certain disclosures; deletion demands sent to third parties; a **data retention schedule**. The data shared had included email addresses, IPs and questionnaire answers sent to Facebook, Snapchat, Criteo and Pinterest. [search]
- **Implication:** this matches the spec's "no ad pixels or trackers, never sell or share." Extend it to the marketing site and the Stripe return pages: **no Meta, TikTok or Google Ads pixels and no third-party analytics SDKs on any page tied to the service.** Adopt and publish a retention schedule.

## 4. Messaging rules: TCPA, CTIA, state law, Apple

### 4.1 Does the TCPA reach our iMessage traffic?
- **47 U.S.C. §227 and 47 CFR 64.1200 (eCFR mirror, snapshot 2025-02-06):** §64.1200(a)(1)(iii) bars any call "using an automatic telephone dialing system or an artificial or prerecorded voice" to a number assigned to cellular service, unless it is an emergency or made with **prior express consent**. Under (a)(2), calls that include an advertisement or constitute telemarketing need **prior express written consent**. The FCC treats texts as "calls": (a)(9) states "the term 'call' includes a text message, including a short message service (SMS) call." [primary]
- **ATDS definition, §64.1200(f)(2):** equipment with "the capacity to store or produce telephone numbers to be called using a random or sequential number generator and to dial such numbers." [primary] The Supreme Court's *Facebook v. Duguid* (2021) narrowed autodialer liability this way. [search] Our sends are triggered by user state and are not generated from random or sequential numbers, which reduces 227(b) exposure but does not remove it, and state laws differ.
- **iMessage specifically:** **no court decision found** on whether an iMessage (Apple's data service, addressed to a phone number) is a TCPA "call." The nearest analog is *Derby v. AOL*: AIM messages delivered to phones were not ATDS calls because of human intervention. [search] Circuit trends: the 9th Cir. (Jan 2026) held texts are "calls"; the **7th Cir.** in *Steidinger v. Blackstone Medical Services* (July 2026) held texts are **not** "telephone calls" for the §227(c)(5) Do-Not-Call private right of action, which does not affect §227(b). The **5th Cir.** in *Bradford v. Sovereign Pest Control* (Feb 2026) reportedly rejected the FCC's *written*-consent requirement for telemarketing and held that prior express consent, even oral, suffices. [search] **Treat iMessage as covered** and design to the stricter reading.
- **Damages, 47 U.S.C. §227(b)(3):** actual loss or **$500 per violation**, whichever is greater, which the court may treble if the violation was willful or knowing. §227(c)(5) (DNC): up to $500, trebled if willful. [primary: uscode.house.gov text mirrored in legalize-dev/legalize-us]
- **Users text first.** Our replies within the conversation the user started are "conversational." Proactive check-ins and recaps are informational messages that need **prior express consent**. Any message promoting a purchase, such as a Stripe Checkout link, a trial-ending upsell, or "upgrade to premium", is **telemarketing or an advertisement**, so get **prior express written consent** (the regulatory text still requires it outside the 5th Cir.). [primary text; categorization is my analysis]
- **Telephone solicitation and DNC:** "telephone solicitation" excludes calls to persons with "prior express invitation or permission" (§64.1200(f)(15)(i)). An **established business relationship** (EBR) is based on a purchase within **18 months** or an inquiry within **3 months** (§64.1200(f)(5)). Telemarketers must keep an internal do-not-call list and honor requests within a reasonable time of **≤30 days**, and a DNC request is honored for **5 years** (§64.1200(d)(3), (d)(6)). [primary] **Prior express written consent definition, §64.1200(f)(9), in the Feb-2025 snapshot:** it still contains the FCC's "one-to-one" seller language, which the **11th Cir. vacated on Jan 24, 2025** (*Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC*). The FCC later formally removed it. [search] Either way, we are a single seller.
- **Quiet hours, §64.1200(c)(1):** no telephone solicitation before **8 a.m.** or after **9 p.m.** at the called party's local time. [primary] (c)–(d) apply to telemarketing texts to wireless numbers per §64.1200(e). [primary] **State mini-TCPAs** in **Florida, Oklahoma and Washington** reportedly set **8 a.m.–8 p.m.**, and Connecticut reportedly 9 a.m.–8 p.m. These are for solicitations; whether they apply to texts depends on each statute. [search; UNVERIFIED statutory text]

### 4.2 Revocation and keywords (FCC)
- **2024 Order (FCC 24-24):** consent may be revoked by "any reasonable means." A reply of **"stop," "quit," "end," "revoke," "opt out," "cancel," or "unsubscribe"** is a **per se** reasonable revocation. Other wording also counts if a reasonable person would read it as a revocation. Honor it **as soon as practicable and within 10 business days**. [search; FCC-24-24A1]
- **One confirmation text, §64.1200(a)(12):** it must "merely confirm" the revocation, contain **no marketing**, and be the **only** additional message. If sent within **5 minutes** it is presumed within consent; later than that, we must show the delay was reasonable. If the user had consented to several categories, the confirmation may ask whether the revocation covers all of them, and we must stop every consent-requiring message until the user clarifies. [primary]
- **"Revoke-all" saga:** the original revoke-all rule (one STOP revokes all robocall and robotext categories from that sender) was delayed from 2025-04-11 to 2026-04-11, then to 2027-01-31. It was then **replaced by FCC 26-67** (adopted 2026-09-30). For **informational** calls and texts, a revocation may be treated as applying only to **that category**. Callers may designate an **FCC-approved exclusive opt-out method**. **Telemarketing stays revoke-all.** An FNPRM asks about cutting 10 business days to **7** and shortening the standard keyword list. [search] **Conflict:** one source says it was published in the FR on 2026-10-26 and takes effect **2026-11-25**; another says it takes effect 30 days after a publication that had not yet happened. Re-check.
- **Sendblue cross-reference:** Sendblue has built-in opt-out detection for stop, unsubscribe, cancel, opt out, revoke, end and quit. Recipients it has opted out are blocked at the provider. See `docs/research/imessage-providers.md`. Our own state machine must stay the source of truth and stay in sync with the provider's flag.

### 4.3 CTIA Messaging Principles and Best Practices
- CTIA's framework covers carrier messaging (SMS/MMS). I found **no source** on whether it covers Apple iMessage, which runs over Apple's network rather than the carrier's. [search] **However, Sendblue falls back from iMessage to SMS** when the recipient lacks iMessage (`was_downgraded`) (see `imessage-providers.md`). Downgraded traffic is carrier SMS, so CTIA and carrier rules apply to it.
- **Consent tiers, from vendor summaries of CTIA (2019 table):** **Conversational** (the consumer starts the exchange and the business only replies) needs implied consent. **Informational** (the consumer gives a number for non-promotional follow-up) needs express consent; sources disagree on whether it must be written. **Promotional** needs express written consent, and adding a call to action can make a message promotional. [search]
- **Keywords and messages:** STOP plus common synonyms (UNSUBSCRIBE, CANCEL, END, QUIT) and plain-language requests. **One final opt-out confirmation.** HELP returns the program name and a customer-care contact. The opt-in confirmation should state the program name, message frequency, opt-out instructions and customer care. Remind people periodically how to opt out. [search; official CTIA 2017 PDF title only]
- **Records:** keep a one-to-one record of every opt-in: timestamp, medium, the exact experience shown, the campaign, and the identity of the person. Consent is not transferable across channels or lists. [search: vendor guidance]

### 4.4 Washington commercial-text statute (RCW 19.190)
- **RCW 19.190.060:** no person doing business in WA may "initiate or assist in the transmission of an electronic commercial text message" to a WA resident's cellular number. "**Electronic text message**" means "a text message sent to a cellular telephone ... equipped with short message service **or any similar capability**" (19.190.010(6)). A "commercial electronic text message" is one "sent to promote real property, goods, or services for sale or lease" (.010(3)). **This is allowed if** "the subscriber has clearly and affirmatively consented in advance" (.070(1)(b)). Damages are **$500 per message** or actual damages, whichever is greater (.040(1)), plus CPA per se liability (.060(2)). [primary: wa-law.org RCW mirror. The mirror shows history through 2005, so **check app.leg.wa.gov for later amendments**.]
- **Implication:** before any Stripe or upgrade text to a user who may be in WA, get **separate, affirmative, logged consent to promotional texts**, or send the checkout link only in reply to an explicit user request ("how do I subscribe?"). Treating that reply as conversational is my interpretation.

Apple "won't approve a deployment using virtual agents exclusively"; a **live-agent escalation path is mandatory**. Messages must be sent under the business's own brand, and Business Updates have opt-in disclosure requirements. [search: register.apple.com] AMB is also customer-initiated and identified by brand, not by phone number. So **our bot-only, phone-number product does not fit AMB as described.**

Sendblue says it complies with Apple's ToS (a vendor claim). A competitor says Apple's ToS do not explicitly authorize this kind of automation. ## 5. Age: COPPA and 18+ gating
- **COPPA, 16 CFR 312 (pre-2025 text, eCFR mirror):** "Child" means under **13**. The rule applies to operators of online services "directed to children" **or** with "**actual knowledge** that it is collecting or maintaining personal information from a child" (§312.3). Personal information includes "**a telephone number**," online contact information (including "an instant messaging user identifier"), persistent identifiers, and "a **photograph** ... where such file contains a child's image or voice" (§312.2). Operators must give notice, obtain **verifiable parental consent before any collection**, offer parental review and deletion, and maintain security (§312.3(a)–(e)). [primary]
- **2025 amendments:** published in the FR on 2025-04-22 (doc 2025-05904), **effective 2025-06-23**, with most compliance due **2026-04-22**. They add mixed-audience rules, separate parental consent for third-party ad disclosures, a "text plus" consent method, a definition of mobile numbers as online contact information **only** for obtaining parental consent, and biometric identifiers and government IDs as personal information. [search]
- **Implications for an iMessage service:** we would collect a child's phone number and photos the moment they text us. We do not direct the service to children. Once we have actual knowledge (a user says they are 12), COPPA obligations attach. **Design:** the onboarding age gate comes before any health data is stored. If the user indicates they are under 18, send a polite refusal, stop processing, and **delete everything collected** (including media), keeping only a minimal suppression record (see Risks).
- **Why 18+ and not 13+:** the spec requires it. ED risk and the safety of calorie targets differ for adolescents, and the clinical floors in §7 are adult figures. It also avoids teen-specific state laws, which I did not research. Nevada SB 63 (2025, children's data) reportedly was not enacted. [search; UNVERIFIED]
- **Recording age confirmation:** store an `age_attestation` row with: the outbound prompt template ID and exact text, the inbound reply's provider message ID, the parsed result (`attested_18_plus` / `under_18` / `unclear`), the method (`self_attestation_reply`), the timestamp, and the policy version. Use a neutral question ("What year were you born?" or "How old are you?") rather than a leading "Reply YES if 18+." That is my recommendation; I could not retrieve the FTC's neutral-age-screen guidance this session (**UNVERIFIED**). Store only the derived 18+ boolean and the birth year if asked, not a full date of birth.

## 6. HIPAA does not apply
- **45 CFR 160.103 (eCFR mirror):** a **covered entity** is (1) a health plan, (2) a health care clearinghouse, or (3) "a health care provider **who transmits any health information in electronic form in connection with a transaction covered by this subchapter**" (standard transactions such as insurance billing). [primary] We bill no insurer and contract with no covered entity, so we are neither a covered entity nor a business associate.
- **HHS OCR, "Health App Use Scenarios & HIPAA" (Feb 2016):** a developer of a consumer-chosen app, where the consumer enters their own data and shares it with a provider only at their own direction, is **not a business associate**. A developer becomes a BA only when it builds or offers the app **for** a covered entity. HHS's tracking guidance adds that HIPAA does not protect information users enter into apps not offered by or on behalf of regulated entities. [search: hhs.gov]
- **The HBNR covers the gap:** it "does not apply to HIPAA-covered entities, or to any other entity to the extent that it engages in activities as a business associate" (16 CFR 318.1(a)). [primary] The MHMDA legislative findings say the same (Sec. 2(2)). [primary]
- **Watch-outs:** HIPAA could attach if we later sell to employers' health plans, clinics or dietitian practices **on their behalf**, or employ providers who bill insurance electronically.

### 7.4 Warning signs a text classifier can look for
These come from NEDA, ANAD and Alliance pages. They are not a diagnostic checklist, and no one shows them all. [search]
- Preoccupation with weight loss, food, calories and dieting; cutting out whole food groups.
- **Skipping meals** or eating only tiny portions; **fasting**.
- **Compensatory behaviors:** self-induced vomiting; misuse of **laxatives, diet pills or diuretics**; **excessive exercise**, including exercising despite illness or injury, or distress, guilt or anxiety when unable to exercise.
- Physical signs reported in passing: dizziness on standing, fainting, always feeling cold, sleep problems, menstrual irregularities.
- **Product-side signals we can compute without any clinical claim:** requests for targets below the floor; logged intake below the floor for ≥N consecutive days; a goal weight that implies underweight BMI; requested loss rates above the cap; repeated "I ate too much, how do I burn it off" or "purge" phrasing; binge or loss-of-control language. **Design:** run a cheap MODEL_FAST classifier on every inbound turn, plus deterministic keyword rules for "vomit," "purge," "laxative," "diuretic," "diet pills," "starve," "fasting for N days" and similar. When it fires: switch to care mode (no numbers, no praise of restriction), share the §7.1 resources, set a `safety_flag` with a timestamp, and suppress proactive tracking messages until the owner-configured cool-down ends. Store only the flag and category, not the message text.

### 7.5 FDA general-wellness boundary
- **"General Wellness: Policy for Low Risk Devices," final guidance re-issued Jan 6, 2026** (supersedes Sept 27, 2019; town hall Feb 11, 2026). Category 1 claims include **weight management**, physical fitness, stress and sleep. Category 2 claims may say a healthy lifestyle "**may help reduce the risk of**" or "**may help living well with**" a chronic condition. Example of an allowed claim: calorie-tracking software that "helps you manage a healthy eating plan to maintain a healthy weight and balanced diet," which "may help living well with high blood pressure and type II diabetes." A food-logging app for weight management is not a device function. [search: fda.gov guidance and town hall]
- **Outside general wellness, so device territory:** claims to **treat or diagnose obesity**, or to **treat an eating disorder such as anorexia**, or an anxiety disorder; labeling, UI or functionality that references specific diseases, **clinical thresholds**, or **treatment guidance**, or that characterizes output as "**abnormal, pathologic, or diagnostic**"; clinical-action alerts. [search: fda.gov] FDA also issued a separate guidance on Mar 13, 2026, "Medical Devices with Indications Associated with Weight Loss." [search]
- **Statute:** the Cures Act §3060(a) amended FD&C Act §520(o)(1)(B) to exclude software "intended for maintaining or encouraging a healthy lifestyle" that is "unrelated to the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, prevention, or treatment of a disease or condition" from the device definition. [search: fda.gov paraphrase]
- **What the agent must not say:** "treat," "cure," "manage your diabetes," "reverse," "diagnose," "your levels are abnormal," "this will lower your blood pressure," "safe for your kidney disease." It must not give medication or dose advice, or tell users to change prescribed therapy. Supplement UL flags should be phrased as general information: "this totals more than the established adult upper limit for X; check with a pharmacist or doctor," not "this is dangerous for you." The spec's pharmacist or doctor redirect for meds, pregnancy and conditions fits this.

## Implications for our design
1. **Onboarding order (first message from an unknown number):** (a) send the identity line ("AI coach, not a doctor") and the neutral age question. (b) After an 18+ answer, send the consent prompt, which itself lists the four MHMDA elements: categories (meals, photos, weight, goals, supplements, health mentions), purposes, recipients by category ("service providers that run this service, like our AI and messaging providers"), and how to withdraw. Add the privacy policy link and a **separate** CHD notice link, and end with "Reply YES to agree." (c) Collect the profile only after YES. Before YES, do not persist health content. Answer with a short "I need your OK first" and keep only the minimal onboarding state.
2. **No bundling:** consent text must not live inside general Terms. Keep separate consent types for proactive messages per category, and for promotional texts.
3. **Processors only:** sign DPAs or binding contracts with every processor before launch. List them in the CHD notice. Any new recipient or purpose means a new policy version and fresh consent.
4. **Rights handling over iMessage:** add a `MY DATA` (access/export) command alongside DELETE, plus a withdraw-consent path (for example `STOP DATA`, or DELETE). Deletion propagates to the DB, Blob media, logs, the provider (Sendblue message logs or contacts), Anthropic (if anything is retained), and Stripe (only what's legally deletable). Backups must age out within ≤6 months; set Postgres PITR and backup retention below 6 months. Run a 45-day SLA timer, an appeal path, and a link to the WA AG complaint page on denial.
5. **Keyword engine (deterministic, case-insensitive, whole-message):** STOP, QUIT, END, REVOKE, OPT OUT, OPTOUT, CANCEL, UNSUBSCRIBE (and STOPALL) pause all outbound except **one** non-marketing confirmation sent within 5 minutes. START and UNSTOP resume. HELP returns the program name, what we send, the opt-out instructions, the support contact, and the privacy and CHD links. A secondary classifier catches natural-language opt-outs ("stop texting me"). The spec's DELETE needs a confirmation step. Mirror the state to the provider's opt-out flag.
6. **Proactive messages:** opt-in per category. The opt-in confirmation states the frequency and how to stop. Default quiet hours are **8 p.m.–8 a.m. local** (stricter than the spec's 9 p.m., to match the reported FL, OK and WA mini-TCPA windows). Send nothing until the timezone is known. Keep the spec's caps and back-off.
7. **Promotional texts (Stripe, M7):** do not send checkout links or trial-ending upsells unprompted unless `promotional_texts` consent exists (TCPA written consent and RCW 19.190.070). Otherwise, send the link only when the user asks.
8. **HBNR readiness:** treat ourselves as a PHR vendor. Keep a written breach runbook, the 60-day clock, FTC form, media list and substitute-notice plan (homepage banner plus a toll-free number). Contracts must require processors to notify us of breaches (§318.3(b)), and we must tell them we are subject to the HBNR.
9. **No trackers anywhere** (site, Stripe return pages, /dev, /admin). No third-party analytics. Message bodies and photos stay out of logs and error trackers. Publish a retention schedule.
10. **Safety:** keep floors and caps in config. Run the ED classifier on each turn, switch to care mode, and keep resource text with a verified date. Keep agent wording within wellness claims. Enforce the "no praise of restriction" rule with prompt tests and evals.

## Risks
- **The legal text was mostly read through mirrors or search summaries.** The WA session law (mirror) and the HBNR, TCPA and COPPA (pre-2025) and HIPAA definitions (eCFR mirror, 2025-02-06 snapshot) are solid. **Everything in Nevada is [search] only**, and the backup-delay figure (6 months vs 2 years), the penalty, and the full .495 list conflict or are missing. FCC 26-67's effective date conflicts between sources.
- **iMessage legal status is unsettled.** There is no case law, and circuit splits exist on texts as "calls" (9th vs 7th Cir.) and on written consent (5th Cir.). The plan is to design to the strictest reading.
- **Apple platform risk:** third-party iMessage lines are unofficial and Apple can cut them off (the Beeper precedent). SMS fallback brings in CTIA and carrier rules, and possibly 10DLC registration. Check the provider notes.
- **Processor deletion gaps:** per `docs/research/anthropic.md`, Anthropic retains no prompts by default for the Sonnet and Haiku 5.5 models, but **flagged content can be kept up to 2 years**. That may conflict with MHMDA's duty that processors delete. Consider ZDR or a contract term. Sendblue message logs and media URLs (30-day expiry) also need a deletion path.
- **CPA private right of action** in WA, plus the Amazon class-action precedent, mean consent UX defects (bundling, pre-checked or implied consent, LLM-inferred "yes") are litigation risks.
- **Breach notification:** we hold only phone numbers, so individual notice under §318.5 is impractical and substitute notice (public) is likely. A breach is reputationally severe.
- **Clinical thresholds** are general-population adult heuristics from sources of mixed authority (Harvard Health is consumer-facing). The men's NHLBI range and the BMI cut-off are unverified. Misclassification in either direction (missing ED signals, or wrongly flagging athletes) is likely, so expect tuning.
- **Helpline details change** (NEDA's closure is the precedent). The number and hours were verified only via the Alliance's own pages through search.
- **WA RCW 19.190 mirror** shows amendments through 2005 only, so later changes may exist.

**1. Safety-critical values that are still unverified. Do not hard-code them until checked on the live page.**
- **National Alliance for Eating Disorders helpline.** The note has 1-866-662-1235, and also reports an Alliance page showing 1-866-**622**-1235. Both are [search]. Because SPEC requires sharing this helpline, a wrong digit is a safety defect. Before milestone 4 ships, someone must open https://www.allianceforeatingdisorders.com/ and record the number, hours and `verified_at`. Keep the value in config, not in the prompt.
- **Underweight BMI cut-off** for refusing loss goals (§7.3): the note marks it unverified. Get it from CDC or NIH before coding. Until then, don't ship a BMI-based rule.
- **Calorie floors and the loss cap** (§7.2–7.3) are all [search], partly consumer-facing sources. The owner reviews them anyway (SPEC). Keep them as config with the source URL next to each value.

**2. Still-open legal facts that affect launch copy (not the architecture).**
- Nevada NRS 603A.495: the full privacy-policy element list.
- The NV backup-deletion window (6 months vs 2 years).
- FCC 26-67 effective date. Sources conflict between 2026-11-25 and "30 days after a publication not yet made", which matters because that date is about four weeks away.
- WA RCW 19.190 amendments after 2005.
- The CTIA 2023 text.
- The FTC neutral age-screen guidance.

**3. Cross-note contradiction: persisting messages before consent.**
- §Implications 1 says "Before YES, do not persist health content."
- database.md §5 (dedupe on `inbound_messages` before returning 200, a `process_after` debounce) and vercel-next.md §Implications 3 store each inbound message's text and media reference in Postgres so a later invocation can process the burst. Serverless debounce **needs** that persistence.
- A new user's first text is often a meal photo, so as designed, health content gets stored, and possibly downloaded and sent to Anthropic, before consent.
- **Proposed resolution (log in DECISIONS.md):** for a user without a `chd_collection` grant, the inbound row keeps only `(provider, provider_message_id, received_at, handle_hmac)`. Message text is kept only long enough to parse onboarding keywords (YES, an age answer, STOP or HELP) and is then nulled. Media is **not** downloaded and **not** sent to the model; reply "Got your photo — I need your OK first, then send it again."
- Whether MHMDA Sec. 5(1)(a)(ii) ("necessary to provide a product or service that the consumer… requested") would allow more is a counsel question. The conservative default above needs no owner input.

**4. Cross-note gap: deletion at processors (§1.5, "notify all processors… must also delete").**
- **Sendblue** has no API to delete messages or media, only `DELETE /api/v2/contacts/{phone}` [sdk-source; imessage-providers.md, Critic addendum §3].
- **Anthropic** may keep flagged content for up to 2 years (anthropic.md §2).
- **Stripe** keeps required financial records (stripe.md §5).
- So the "processors_notified / deletion_confirmed_at" design in §8.2 needs a manual or DPA path for Sendblue and Anthropic. The privacy notice must not promise more than that.
- **Conflict:** deleting the Sendblue contact may also erase Sendblue's STOP record (the same recipient record). That clashes with keeping opt-out proof for 5+ years (§8.1). Keep our own suppression record (open question 2), and re-apply the provider opt-out after any contact deletion.

**5. Cross-note tension: the STOP confirmation.** §4.2 allows exactly one confirmation within 5 minutes. Sendblue blocks sends to opted-out numbers, and may send its own confirmation (unknown). Default: attempt once, and treat `OPTED_OUT` as already confirmed. See imessage-providers.md, Critic addendum §6.

**6. Deviation from SPEC to log.** §Implications 6 sets default quiet hours to **8 p.m.–8 a.m.**, while SPEC says 9 p.m.–8 a.m. The stricter value rests on [search]-only reports of the FL, OK and WA mini-TCPA windows. It is a sensible default (stricter), so per SPEC it is logged in DECISIONS.md rather than asked, along with a note that the statutory text is unverified.
