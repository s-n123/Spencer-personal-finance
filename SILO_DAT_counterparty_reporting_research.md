## 1. Bottom line

Neither Notice 2005-13 (SILO) nor Notice 2008-34 (DAT) subjects the accommodation party — the tax-indifferent lessee in a SILO, or the tax-indifferent contributor and the trustee in a DAT — to any disclosure, list-maintenance or penalty regime by reason of that role. Each notice does three things only: it identifies the transaction and "substantially similar transactions, as listed transactions for purposes of § 1.6011-4(b)(2) of the Income Tax Regulations and §§ 6111 and 6112 of the Internal Revenue Code" [V]; it "also alerts parties involved with these transactions to certain responsibilities that may arise from their involvement with these transactions" [V]; and it recites the generic penalty paragraph (§ 6707A for participants, § 6707(a) and § 6708(a) for material advisors, §§ 6662 and 6662A) [V for 2008-34; not retrieved for 2005-13]. Neither notice uses the authority in Treas. Reg. § 1.6011-4(c)(3)(i)(A) to designate "other types or classes of persons" as participants, and neither names the lessee, contributor, trustee, lender, defeasance bank or any other counterparty. The accommodation party's exposure therefore turns entirely on the general rules: participant status only if its own return reflects the listed tax consequences or strategy, or it knows or has reason to know that its own tax benefits derive from them; material-advisor status only if it makes a "tax statement" and derives fees above the threshold; and §§ 6700/6701 only on a false statement or knowledge of an understatement. The outline's premise is not supported by the listing guidance. The examples that do support "a counterparty becoming subject to the rules" are the basket-contract notices (counterparty expressly a participant) and the intermediary-transaction notices (intermediary and buyer expressly addressed), in section 5.

## 2. How a counterparty can become subject to the reportable transaction rules

**Participant (§ 6011; Form 8886).** Treas. Reg. § 1.6011-4(c)(3)(i)(A), current text:

> "Listed transactions. A taxpayer has participated in a listed transaction if the taxpayer's tax return reflects tax consequences or a tax strategy described in the published guidance that lists the transaction under paragraph (b)(2) of this section. A taxpayer also has participated in a listed transaction if the taxpayer knows or has reason to know that the taxpayer's tax benefits are derived directly or indirectly from tax consequences or a tax strategy described in published guidance that lists a transaction under paragraph (b)(2) of this section. Published guidance may identify other types or classes of persons that will be treated as participants in a listed transaction. Published guidance also may identify types or classes of persons that will not be treated as participants in a listed transaction." [V, each sentence; the position of "also" in the last two sentences came back inconsistently across two extractions — confirm]

Paragraphs (B) and (C) tie participation in confidential and contractual-protection transactions to a return that "reflects a tax benefit from the transaction" plus the confidentiality limitation or the fee-refund right [(B) V; (C) P]; paragraph (E) makes a person a participant in a transaction of interest only if "the taxpayer is one of the types or classes of persons identified as participants in the transaction in the published guidance describing the transaction of interest" [V]. The regulation's source credit is T.D. 9350, 72 FR 43146 (Aug. 3, 2007) [V]. The "types or classes" sentences were proposed in REG-103038-05, 71 FR 64488 (Nov. 2, 2006) and adopted in T.D. 9350; the 2003 regulations (T.D. 9046) had only the first two sentences [U — consistent with your belief; I could not read either preamble, though the T.D. 9350 preamble does record commentators asking that TOI "participation" be "defined in the regulations rather than in the published guidance identifying the transaction of interest" [P]]. The Form 8886 instructions (Rev. Oct. 2022) restate the test as three alternatives, the third being "You are in a type or class of individuals or entities that published guidance treats as participants in a listed transaction" [V]. A lender or trading counterparty whose return does not reflect the listed consequences and that derives no tax benefit from them is not a participant unless the listing guidance names its class.

**Material advisor (§§ 6111, 6112; Form 8918; lists).** § 6111(b)(1)(A) defines a material advisor as a person "who provides any material aid, assistance, or advice with respect to organizing, managing, promoting, selling, implementing, insuring, or carrying out any reportable transaction" and "who directly or indirectly derives gross income in excess of the threshold amount" [P]; the threshold is "$50,000 in the case of a reportable transaction substantially all of the tax benefits from which are provided to natural persons, and $250,000 in any other case" [V]. § 301.6111-3(b)(1) repeats the definition [V] and adds: "A material advisor generally does not include a person who makes a tax statement solely in the person's capacity as an employee, shareholder, partner or agent of another person" [V]. § 301.6111-3(b)(2)(i): "Except as provided in paragraph (b)(5) of this section, a person provides material aid, assistance, or advice … if the person makes or provides a tax statement to or for the benefit of" a taxpayer or material advisor required (or that would be required) to disclose [P]. § 301.6111-3(b)(2)(ii)(A): "A tax statement is any statement (including another person's statement), oral or written, that relates to a tax aspect of a transaction that causes the transaction to be a reportable transaction as defined in § 1.6011-4(b)(2) through (7) of this chapter. A tax statement under this section includes tax result protection that insures some or all of the tax benefits of a reportable transaction." [V] Thresholds are reduced for listed transactions to $10,000 and $25,000 [P]. Paragraph (b)(4) makes a person a material advisor only when the aid, the fee and the taxpayer's entry into the transaction have all occurred, and on a later listing, on the listing date [P]. Paragraph (b)(5) lets published guidance identify "other types or classes of persons" as material advisors [P; exact wording not retrieved]. I found no examples in § 301.6111-3(b) in any extraction (eCFR, LII, GovInfo 2012/2016/2021) and none involving a party who merely enters into a transaction; I believe the section has none — confirm. § 301.6112-1(a): "Each material advisor, as defined in § 301.6111-3(b), with respect to any reportable transaction, as defined in § 1.6011-4(b) of this chapter, shall prepare and maintain a list …" [V]; § 6112(a) is quoted in section 7. A counterparty that merely enters into the transaction for ordinary commercial compensation is not a material advisor unless it "makes or provides a tax statement"; the pressure points for a bank are tax language in term sheets or confirmations, passing along "another person's statement," "tax result protection," and a notice invoking (b)(5).

**Penalties by role.**

## 3. SILO — Notice 2005-13, "Tax-Exempt Leasing Involving Defeasance"

**Cite and status.** 2005-9 I.R.B. 630 [V via Tax Notes; the IRS Appeals guideline cites "2005-1 C.B. 630 (Feb. 28, 2005)"]. Released February 11, 2005 with Treasury release JS-2249 [V]; listed "effective February 11, 2005, the date this notice was released to the public" [P]. Still on the IRS "Listed transactions" page as "Notice 2005-13 - Sale-In Lease-Out transactions" [V]. Not among the nine items obsoleted by Notice 2025-22 [V]; no hit in Notices 2025-36 or 2026-58, whose full lists I could not read [P]. Section 470 (AJCA; leases entered into after March 12, 2004 [V]) governs later deals; the Treasury release says Congress "limited deductions for losses from future SILO transactions" and the notice targets benefits "claimed on earlier SILO deals" [P]. Whether the notice itself mentions § 470: not retrieved [U]. No APA challenge to this notice surfaced.

**Text.** Opening: "… a taxpayer enters into a purported sale-leaseback arrangement with a tax-indifferent person in which substantially all of the tax-indifferent person's payment obligations are economically defeased and the taxpayer's risk of loss from a decline, and opportunity for profit from an increase, in the value of the leased property are limited." [P; the two internal phrases V] "This notice alerts taxpayers and their representatives that these transactions are tax avoidance transactions and identifies these transactions, and substantially similar transactions, as listed transactions for purposes of § 1.6011-4(b)(2) of the Income Tax Regulations and §§ 6111 and 6112 of the Internal Revenue Code." [P] "This notice also alerts parties involved with these transactions to certain responsibilities that may arise from their involvement with these transactions." [V] Analysis: "In substance, the transactions described above are merely a transfer of tax benefits to X, coupled with X's investment of the Equity Collateral for a predetermined after-tax rate of return." [V via Tax Notes] So the notice calls the U.S. taxpayer-lessor "X" and defines "Equity Collateral"; the designation for the tax-indifferent person was not retrieved [U].

**Disclosure and penalty paragraph: not retrieved** despite seven phrase searches; it must be quoted from the PDF. The Treasury release says "Taxpayers who enter into SILOs and who are required to file tax returns must disclose their participation to the IRS" and "Promoters of listed transactions must keep lists of investors and, in certain cases, register those transactions with the IRS" [V]. The June 29, 2005 Coordinated Issue Paper and the Appeals Settlement Guideline (UIL 9300.38-00, eff. Jan. 31, 2008) frame the penalty issue as "Whether § 6662 … § 6662A … or § 6707A … apply to SILO transactions" [V].

**The accommodation party.** Foreign or domestic tax-indifferent owners (transit agencies, municipalities, utilities) that "sold" rolling stock or similar assets and leased them back; most of the price was placed with a bank under payment-undertaking agreements and in high-grade securities (the "Equity Collateral"); the lessee kept an "accommodation fee" of about 4 to 8 percent of asset value [secondary]. CCA 200712044 refers to "payment undertaking agreements" and "the tax-indifferent parties' purchase options" [V]; AM 2007-008 describes "X, a U.S. taxpayer, [that] purports to acquire … an ownership interest (SILO transaction) in property owned by Y, a tax-exempt entity" [V]. The defeasance and payment-undertaking banks are a second facilitating class.

**Reach.** The notice names no class of participants, and every IRS material applying it (CCA 200712044; AM 2007-008; the CIP; the Appeals guideline; the August 2008 LILO/SILO settlement initiative and IR-2008-121; the sample closing-agreement letters) addresses only the lessor's deductions. The only IRS-document hits for "accommodation" are the sample settlement letter's basis formula, "equity collateral + accommodation fees" [V via Tax Notes], and the Appeals guideline's reference to a 2005 industry directive on OID, accommodation fees and transaction costs [P]. No IRS document returned a hit for "facilitat" or "accommodation party." The lessee files no U.S. return reflecting the listed benefits (a domestic transit agency is not a taxpayer at all), so § 1.6011-4(c)(3)(i)(A) is not triggered; a defeasance bank would be a material advisor only on the general tax-statement test.

## 4. DAT — Notice 2008-34, "Distressed Asset Trust Transactions"

**Cite and status.** 2008-12 I.R.B. 645 [V]; released February 27, 2008; listed for transactions "entered into after October 22, 2004" [V] (pre-AJCA partnership variants are covered by a separate coordinated issue paper; the DAT was made a coordinated issue effective February 27, 2008 [V]). Still listed as "Notice 2008-34 - Distressed Asset Trust (DAT) Transaction" [V]; not in Notice 2025-22 [V]; no hit in Notices 2025-36 or 2026-58 [P]. Applied in CCA 200936028 (July 15, 2009) [V]. No APA challenge surfaced.

**Text.** Opening: "… a tax indifferent party, directly or indirectly, contributes one or more distressed assets (for example, a creditor's interest in debt) with a high basis and low fair market value to a trust or series of trusts and sub-trusts, and a U.S. taxpayer acquires an interest in the trust … for the purpose of shifting a built-in loss from the tax indifferent party to the U.S. taxpayer that has not incurred the economic loss." [P; first clause V] The second and third sentences are the same listing and "parties involved … responsibilities" sentences as in Notice 2005-13 [V]. Facts, as recited in the notice and repeated in the Coordinated Issue Paper [P as to which document carries each sentence]: "a tax indifferent party creates a trust (Main-Trust) with trustee X. The tax indifferent party contributes distressed assets directly or indirectly (through a partnership or otherwise) to Main-Trust" and is "the grantor and beneficiary of Main-Trust"; "A U.S. taxpayer (Taxpayer) transfers cash or a note (approximately equaling the fair market value of the distressed asset) to Main-Trust in exchange for certificates evidencing units of beneficial interest in Main-Trust"; the trustee "creates a separate sub-trust (Sub-Trust), transfers certificates evidencing units of beneficial interest in Sub-Trust (Sub-Trust Certificates) to Taxpayer, and allocates the distressed assets to Sub-Trust for the sole benefit of the beneficiary of the Sub-Trust." The CIP adds that the tax-indifferent party, "through a U.S. promoter, solicits a U.S. taxpayer" and "Promoter fees are generally charged based on the anticipated tax benefit" [V].

**Disclosure and penalty paragraph** (extracted from the PDF; the search tool warned that line breaks were garbled, so confirm punctuation): "Transactions that are the same as, or substantially similar to, the transaction described in this notice that are entered into after October 22, 2004, are identified as 'listed transactions' for purposes of § 1.6011-4(b)(2) and §§ 6111 and 6112 effective February 27, 2008, the date this notice was released to the public." [P] "Independent of their classification as 'listed transactions,' transactions that are the same as, or substantially similar to, the transaction described in this notice may already be subject to the requirements of §§ 6011, 6111, or 6112 …" [P] "Persons required to disclose these transactions under § 1.6011-4 who fail to do so may be subject to the penalty under § 6707A." [P] "Persons required to disclose these transactions under § 6111 who fail to do so may be subject to the penalty under § 6707(a)." [V] "Persons required to maintain lists of investors under § 6112 who fail to do so (or who fail to provide such lists when requested by the Service) may be subject to the penalty under § 6708(a)." [V] "In addition, the Service may impose other penalties on persons involved in these transactions or substantially similar transactions, including the accuracy-related penalty under § 6662 or § 6662A." [V]

**Reach.** There is no "participants" paragraph. CCA 200936028 resolves participation for the U.S. taxpayer under the general rule: the taxpayer "participated in the DAT transaction in 2005, because the taxpayer's Form 1040 reflected the tax consequences or tax strategy described in Notice 2008-34," with the duty arising only on the February 27, 2008 listing [P]. The tax-indifferent party (typically foreign) files no return reflecting the loss; the trustee acts for the trust; the promoter is reached, if at all, through the material-advisor test and § 6700. One nuance: Main-Trust or Sub-Trust (and so the trustee as filer) could be a participant if a trust return reflected the loss — exposure derived from the trust's return position, not from "facilitation."

## 5. Comparison points

**(a) Basket contracts.** Notices 2015-47 (listed) and 2015-48 (transaction of interest), 2015-30 I.R.B. 76 and 77 (July 8, 2015), were revoked on October 21, 2015 by Notices 2015-73 and 2015-74, 2015-46 I.R.B. 660 and 663 [V]. All four treat the counterparty as a participant. Notice 2015-74: "Under § 1.6011-4(c)(3)(i)(E), for each year in which a transaction described in this notice is open, only the following parties are treated as participating in the transaction of interest: …" — a closed list of the purchaser, any general partner of a purchaser partnership, any managing member of a purchaser LLC, and the counterparty [P]; Notice 2015-73 has the parallel list under (c)(3)(i)(A) [P]. KPMG's 2015 description of 2015-47/48: "The notices treat the following persons as having participated in a basket option or a basket contract: (1) the purchaser; (2) if the purchaser is a partnership, any general partner of the purchaser; (3) if the purchaser is a limited liability company, any managing member of the purchaser; and (4) the counterparty to the derivative." [V, secondary] The notices require disclosure "by participants and material advisors" [P]; I found no sentence deeming the counterparty a material advisor, so your recollection on that point is unconfirmed [U]. Status: Notice 2015-74 was obsoleted by the July 12, 2024 proposed regulations (REG-102161-23, 89 FR 57111), which would re-list both sets of transactions under proposed § 1.6011-16 and keep the counterparty as a participant subject to two counterparty-only exceptions (the taxpayer represents under penalties of perjury that no return reflects a tax benefit; or the counterparty establishes that the taxpayer is a nonresident alien or foreign corporation not engaged in a U.S. trade or business) [P]. Notice 2015-73 was obsoleted by Notice 2025-22 (Apr. 14, 2025) in the Executive Order 14219 clean-up [V, secondary]. Whether § 1.6011-16 has been finalized is unconfirmed (section 8).

**(b) Intermediary transactions.** Notice 2008-20 (2008-6 I.R.B. 406) was issued for "identifying the persons who are treated as participants in an Intermediary Transaction Tax Shelter under § 1.6011-4(c)(3)(i)(A)" [V] and had safe harbors for certain sellers and buyers, including a buyer whose only acquired assets are securities [P]. Notice 2008-111 (2008-51 I.R.B. 1288 [P]; Dec. 2, 2008) superseded it: "A transaction is treated as an Intermediary Transaction with respect to a particular person only if that person engages in the transaction pursuant to a plan, the transaction contains the four objective components indicative of an Intermediary Transaction, and no safe harbor exception applies to that person." [V] "A transaction may be an Intermediary Transaction with respect to one person and not be an Intermediary Transaction with respect to another person." [V] "Pursuant to a plan" turns on knowing or having reason to know, with presumptions for 5-percent shareholders and officers or directors [P]; section 5 safe harbors include a shareholder whose disposed stock is publicly traded [V]; section 6 recites the § 6707A / 6707(a) / 6708(a) paragraph [P]. The role-by-role participant list (seller X, target T, intermediary M, buyer Y) was not retrieved [U]. The point: buyers and the intermediary were brought within participant status only through person-specific knowledge tests and safe harbors.

**(c) Express exclusions of lenders or trading counterparties.** None in the SILO or DAT guidance. Elsewhere: the closed "only the following parties" list in the 2015 basket notices excludes everyone not named (custodians, prime brokers, lenders) [P]; proposed § 1.6011-16's "designee" definition excludes an investment adviser, officer or employee of an entity such as a mutual fund [P]; the 2023 monetized-installment-sale proposal (REG-109348-22, proposed § 1.6011-13) treats the seller, the intermediary and "the purported lender" as participants but "would not treat the buyer of the gain property as a participant," and asked for comment on that choice [P]. Treasury has thus reached a financing or trading counterparty expressly when it wanted to, and has used the "types or classes" power to do it.

## 6. What the outline author probably meant

Moderate confidence: "facilitation party" is the author's synonym for the "accommodation party" or "tax-indifferent party" of SILO/LILO and DAT practice — the foreign or tax-exempt lessee paid an accommodation fee in a SILO, and the tax-indifferent contributor (and loosely the trustee) in a DAT. Those parties facilitate a transfer of tax benefits without claiming any, which makes them the closest structural analogue to BNY lending to or trading with a tax-aware fund. On that reading the premise fails: the notices impose nothing on those parties and no IRS material applying them treats those parties as participants or material advisors. The accurate formulation is the opposite of the outline's: SILO and DAT are examples of listings that left the accommodation party outside the regime, while the basket-contract and intermediary-transaction guidance are the examples of a counterparty being swept in. Low confidence alternative: the author meant the defeasance and payment-undertaking banks in SILOs; the answer is the same, since those banks are reached only by the general material-advisor test. Nothing suggests the author had in mind an IRS document that uses the word "facilitation."
